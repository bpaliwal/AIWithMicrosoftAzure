If your checked bag is **lost or significantly delayed**, the Department of Transportation says the airline must provide a **prompt refund of the fee charged for transporting that bag**. This is based on the rules in the DOT’s **Refunds for Airline Fare and Ancillary Service Fees** and **Refunds and Other Consumer Protections** documents.

A few key points from the Knowledge Base:

- The delay is measured from the time you are given the opportunity to deplane at your **final destination airport** until the bag is delivered to an agreed location, such as your home or hotel, or picked up at the airport.  
  - Source: **Refunds and Other Consumer Protections.pdf**
- For a refund to apply, the passenger generally must file a **Mishandled Baggage Report (MBR)** with the carrier that operated the flight, or for a multi-carrier itinerary, with the carrier that operated the **last segment**.  
  - Source: **Refunds for Airline Fare and Ancillary Service Fees.pdf**
- The DOT also notes that airlines must make every reasonable effort to return mishandled baggage within the timeframes set in the rule for purposes of avoiding refunding baggage fees.  
  - Source: **Refunds and Other Consumer Protections.pdf**
